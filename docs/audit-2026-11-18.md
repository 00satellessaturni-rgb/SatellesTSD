# SatellesTSD — fifth audit, the whole app, and a law-checked simulation of income scenarios (2026-11-18, version 0.34.0, commit cb8381f)

**How it was made.** One workflow, two arms. *The bug hunt:* fourteen Opus finders, one per area (reading files; the TÖR export and contracts; matching with the new phrases overlay; the review; tax computation against the 2026 law; declaring; the XML out and the feedback in; the register lookups; the store; the reports; the self-update; the window with the three new tools; and two cross-cutting sweeps, the monthly flow and data integrity), read-only and told to skip what the four earlier audits record as open, refused or settled. Each finding went to a skeptic told to refute it by tracing the code; a critical or major one also to a second skeptic told to refute it by the plan's decisions and its real effect. 38 findings: 29 confirmed, 0 split, 9 refuted. *The scenario check:* eight families of income scenarios (68 in all) written as simulation files and run through the app's own path (decision 204: import, search, review, build, XML). For each family an independent payroll-tax agent worked out from the 2026 law alone what must be declared, without seeing the app's tax code or its output, verifying the figures at riigiteataja.ee and emta.ee; a third agent compared, and every difference went to a judge who traced the code and the law and ruled: the app wrong, the expectation wrong, or a deliberate plan decision (and whether that decision itself goes against the law). The scenario files, the app's results and XML, and each family's law expectation are kept in the session's scratchpad (sims\\<family>).

Severity: critical = wrong tax or declaration data sent to the tax board, or data loss; major = a flow the accountant needs that breaks or misleads; minor = a wrong message or an edge case with a workaround; nit = cosmetic. The severity is the highest a confirming skeptic gave.

**Status (2026-11-18).** FIXED, plan item 63: item 1 (decision 208), item 2 (206), items 3 and 4 (205), S5 (207), S11–S14 as the Seaded setting "Kaks lepingut korraga", the Tööleping by default (209), O1 (210) and O2 (211). The minors 5–21 and the nits 22–29 are open.

## The findings

### 1. [critical] The employee's unemployment insurance (TSD_TK) is withheld from employees past pension age, and from early-pension recipients, unless the 'Vanaduspensionil' tick is set

tax — `SatellesTSD.Core/Tax/TaxEngine.cs:78`

**Scenario.** Simulator scenario hunt-tax/pensionage.json: a resident with a Tööleping, PensionAgeYear 2025 (already of pension age, not drawing a pension), OnOldAgeOrFlexiblePension false, paid 1000 net on 2026-10-05 as 'palk'. The line goes out as code 10, gross 1102.89, with TSD_TK 17.65 withheld (MVT_650 776 is applied correctly). EMTA's own page on töötuskindlustusmakse says the 1.6 % premium is not withheld from 'vanaduspensionieas isikutele ja isikutele, kellele on määratud ennetähtaegne või paindlik pension'. The obligation ends on the last day of the month in which the person reaches pension age, or in which the pension is granted. The employer's 0.8 % stays. Here 17.65 too much is withheld and declared, the gross is too high by the same, and the income tax is computed on the wrong base.

**Detail.** EffectiveFlags.Of drops TK only on payee.OnOldAgeOrFlexiblePension, a hand tick on the card labelled 'Vanaduspensionil' (UiText.OnPension). The rules notes (UiText.RulesNotes) state the law as 'Vanadus- või paindliku vanaduspensioni saajalt ... ei peeta kinni', and PayeeTaxFacts documents the flag as being on the pension. Both leave out two groups: a person past pension age who does not draw a pension, and an early-pension (ennetähtaegne) recipient. The card already holds PensionAgeYear, which the engine uses for MVT_650 but never for TK. An accountant who follows the card's label and the rules text withholds TK that the law says is not due. The tick is also undated, so the app cannot tell the month the pension was granted (TK is still due that month) from the months after it.

**Verified.** I couldn't refute this. TaxEngine.cs:78 sets `employeeInsurance = employerInsurance && !payee.OnOldAgeOrFlexiblePension`. That flag comes only from the card's hand tick, labelled "Vanaduspensionil" (UiText.OnPension). PensionAgeYear only feeds PensionAgeThisYear, and that is used only for MVT_650 (TaxEngine:96, StoreModel:78/82, MonthAllocator, PaymentChecks). So a person with PensionAgeYear 2025 who has no tick gets TSD_TK on a 2026 salary.

The law is broader than the code. The Unemployment Insurance Act, as EMTA explains it, exempts three groups from the employee's 1.6 %: people of old-age pension age, people granted an early (ennetähtaegne) pension, and people on a flexible pension. KR_TK_2 is EMTA's validation rule, and it only forbids TSD_TK for people actually on a pension. It doesn't make withholding from a pension-age non-pensioner correct; it just doesn't catch it. So the file passes validation but declares TK that isn't due, with a wrong gross and income tax base.

No plan decision covers this. Line 61 of the plan, the reference docs and RulesNotes (UiText:667) all describe the pension case only. The scenario can happen: PensionAgeYear before the period year is a normal card value.

I'm rating it major, not critical. It does put wrong data in the declaration, but there is a workaround: the accountant can set the tick by hand. The label and the rules note both point them away from doing that, so the flow misleads.

**Second skeptic.** I could not refute this. No numbered decision covers TK for a person of pension age. The design table (plan line 113, G6) lists "pensioner (no TK), pension-age (MVT_650)", which matches the code: TaxEngine.cs:78 drops TK only on the hand tick OnOldAgeOrFlexiblePension, and PensionAgeThisYear feeds only MVT_650 (TaxEngine:96, MonthAllocator:66, PaymentChecks:107). That row restates EMTA's validation rule KR_TK_2. It is not a reasoned decision that pension-age employees owe the 1.6 %. As far as I know the law (the Unemployment Insurance Act, as EMTA explains it) exempts people who have reached old-age pension age, early-pension recipients and flexible-pension recipients from the employee's premium. I am relying on that from memory and did not check it against a source. A card with PensionAgeYear before the period year is a normal value, so the engine can be certain here, yet it still withholds and declares TSD_TK. That inflates the gross and gives a wrong base on a file that passes validation. The PayeeTaxFacts doc and the card label "Vanaduspensionil" both tell the accountant to tick only real pensioners, so the workaround is not obvious. I keep it at major, not critical: the hand tick fixes it, the amount is 1.6 % of pay, and it affects only employees past pension age or on an early pension.

**Fix:** Drop the employee's TK when the person has reached pension age by the month before the payment, or when the tick is set. PensionAgeYear < period year is certain; for the year of reaching the age, ask for the month, or note it for the accountant to decide. Rename the tick and the rules note to cover 'vanaduspensioniealine, ennetähtaegse või paindliku pensioni saaja', and say that TK still stops only from the month after the age is reached or the pension is granted.

### 2. [major] A zero line the build cannot compute (NOT_ALLOCATED, NO_PAYEE, the line factory's refusal) leaves the previous build's draft standing; the export sends it under the old code/facts and the export check calls it current

declare — `SatellesTSD.Core/Declare/PeriodDeclarer.cs:637`

**Scenario.** A resident with a valid Tööleping and no pay in the month. The residency was confirmed by hand and the pension came from Pensionikeskus. Koosta read drafts the zero line (type 10, SM 292.38) under personal code A. The accountant corrects the code on the card from A to B (a typo). Decision 180 drops the Pensionikeskus pension and keeps the hand residency. Koosta read again: the zero line goes into monthPayments, and allocator.Allocate throws 'Payment type 10 withholds the funded pension, but the payee's II-pillar status has not been checked'. The NOT_ALLOCATED branch (lines 634-642) only claims the source, so Retire skips it and the R draft under code A stays. On Ekspordi, DraftsCurrent's dry-run build does the same, before == after, current = true, so nothing is asked. The file carries a zero line with SM 292.38 for code A, a code the accountant has just said is wrong. Same with the card's code cleared and no MRR code (hand residency kept): the NO_PAYEE branch (lines 503-511) claims and keeps the draft. Same with A1 set to EstonianA1WorkAbroad before the countries are filled in: DeclarationLineFactory throws in Reconcile, caught at line 660, and the earlier draft stays. Simulator: hunt-declare/s1-zero-pension-null (ZERO_LINE then NOT_ALLOCATED, no line) and s14-zero-a1-nocountries ('minimum: An A1 case needs both...').

**Detail.** Ordinary payments in these states are refused by the review's checks first (PENSION_NOT_CHECKED, NO_PAYEE_ID, A1_COUNTRIES_MISSING), so their drafts are deleted on the refused path. The zero line never passes those checks, so it falls through to these three 'what stands, stands' paths. Each path claims the source and never deletes a Draft. This is the hole decisions 142 and 191 closed for CODE_INVALID and RESIDENCY_NOT_CHECKED ('a draft standing under it goes, since the export takes every draft'), but NO_PAYEE, NOT_ALLOCATED and the per-line catch were left out. The log also says 'ZERO_LINE: ... a zero line of type 10 carries it' just before the refusal, which contradicts what happened.

**Verified.** I could not refute this. The code does what the finding says. In PeriodDeclarer.cs the zero line's only guard before it is added to monthPayments is ResidencyCheckedOn (lines 448-466, decision 191). Nothing checks the pension, the payee or the A1 countries first, the way the review's checks do for ordinary payments. Three branches then claim the source without touching a stored Draft: NO_PAYEE (503-511), the allocator's NOT_ALLOCATED catch (634-642), and the Reconcile catch (656-663). TaxEngine.cs:84 throws InvalidOperationException when the pension is null on a type that withholds it, so the NOT_ALLOCATED path is reached. Compare the CODE_INVALID branch (513-538) and the keptPools NOT_ALLOCATED branch (596-622), which both call DeleteDraft. Changing the code on the card does not remove drafts either: ReviewWorkspace.cs:819-866 only clears the registers' facts and FactsControlDate (decision 180). The export takes every Draft (PeriodSubmitter.cs:74). DraftsCurrent's dry-run build leaves the same draft standing, so before equals after, the result is true, and the accountant is not asked. No plan decision covers this as intended: decisions 142 and 191 settle only CODE_INVALID and RESIDENCY_NOT_CHECKED. The result is a zero line with the SM minimum under a code the accountant has just corrected, or under facts made stale by the A1 change. That is wrong data in the file. I rate it major rather than critical: it needs a particular sequence (a person on the minimum with no pay, a build, then a card change into one of these states, then an export without asking Päringud again), and the build log does show the NOT_ALLOCATED, NO_PAYEE or A1 refusal. That refusal never says the old draft still stands, and the earlier ZERO_LINE note says a line carries the minimum, so the log misleads too. The proposed fix fits: delete a stored Draft on those three paths, as the CODE_INVALID branch does.

**Second skeptic.** I could not refute it. PeriodDeclarer.cs checks only ResidencyCheckedOn before it adds the zero line (lines 449-476). Three later paths then claim the source without deleting a stored Draft: the NO_PAYEE branch (503-511), the allocator's NOT_ALLOCATED catch (634-642) and the per-line Reconcile catch (656-663). A claimed source is skipped by Retire (lines 282-290), so the earlier R draft stays. That draft carries the old payee code from DeclarationLineFactory.Create(..., person.PayeeId, ...), and the export takes every Draft (PeriodSubmitter.cs:74). DraftsCurrent compares before and after a dry-run build, and both still hold the draft, so the export check reports "current" and asks nothing. The trigger is real. ReviewWorkspace.SavePerson (lines 820-864) sets FundedPensionMember to null when the code changes and the pension came from Pensionikeskus, so a type-10 zero line reaches the allocator with no pension and is refused as NOT_ALLOCATED. No plan decision says a stale draft should stand. Decisions 92 and 110 delete drafts on the held NOT_ALLOCATED path. Decisions 142 and 191 delete them for CODE_INVALID and RESIDENCY_NOT_CHECKED. The build's own comment says "since the export takes every draft", and this path breaks that rule. So a corrected-away code can go to MTA on a zero line with the SM minimum. I rate it major rather than critical. It needs a specific sequence (a minimum-carrying person with no pay, a build, a code or A1 change, then an export without asking the registers again), and the build log does show the NOT_ALLOCATED, NO_PAYEE or line refusal. That log still misleads: it says neither that the old draft stands nor that the export will send it.

**Fix:** In the NO_PAYEE branch, the NOT_ALLOCATED catch and the Reconcile catch (lines 656-663), do what the CODE_INVALID branch does: for each claimed source whose bySource entry is a Draft, DeleteDraft and report it as Deleted; keep a line on the server as Kept. Add the ZERO_LINE problem only after the line is actually built, or reword it when the build stops later.

### 3. [major] An M or T refused black over a saved version is invisible: missing from the feedback's status counts and the grid, and passed silently by Märgi kinnitatuks

declare — `SatellesTSD.Core/Declare/PeriodSubmitter.cs:293`

**Scenario.** October was declared and its R lines Accepted. The period is reopened and a salary corrected; Koosta read drafts an M on key ...-01-003. The correction file is exported and uploaded, and MTA refuses that M black (any black code other than _4 or _5), so RecordFeedback sets the M to Rejected. IStore.Payments returns the latest version that is not Rejected for each key, which is the old Accepted R. Then: (1) ReadFeedback counts statuses over Payments(...).Where(p => p.SubmissionId == submissionId), so the refused M is not counted and the log reads e.g. '1 rida;' with no Tagasi lükatud; (2) the Deklaratsioon grid shows the R as accepted with the R's own feedback; (3) MarkConfirmed builds 'refused' from the same Payments list, so the month is confirmed without the question decision 94 asks. MTA still holds the old figures while the review shows the corrected ones. The same happens for a T refused black over an Accepted R: the payment the review no longer declares stays on the server, and nothing says so.

**Detail.** Decision 94: 'a refused line is not on the server, so the month confirmed without it is not the month the review shows.' Audit 2026-11-05 #49 fixed this only for keys whose every version was refused. A refused correction on a saved key is the same case: what the server holds is not what the review shows. The comment at line 290 ('a refused T aside, it took nothing back and nothing resends it') is also wrong in practice. After a reopen, the next build retires the payment again and drafts a new T, so the refused T does matter. Only the history dialog shows the refused version.

**Verified.** I could not refute this. The code works as the finder says. SqliteStore.Payments (SqliteStore.cs:499-506) returns, for each key, the last version that is not Rejected. When an M or T sent over an Accepted R is refused black, RecordFeedback sets it to Rejected (line 697), and Payments then returns the old Accepted R. Three places read only that list:

1. **ReadFeedback** (PeriodSubmitter.cs:349) counts statuses over `Payments(...).Where(p => p.SubmissionId == submissionId)`. The R belongs to the earlier submission, so the refused M is left out. MainForm.cs:1287 then logs "N rida;" with no "Tagasi lükatud" count. Only the line-level messages of the general findings are logged.
2. **PeriodDeclarer.Lines** (PeriodDeclarer.cs:137-150), which fills the grid, shows the R with its own last feedback and status Accepted. Only the Versions count (2) hints at anything more.
3. **MarkConfirmed** (PeriodSubmitter.cs:280-293) builds both `open` and `refused` from the same list, so neither catches the refused M. Drafts is empty because nothing has been rebuilt, so the month is confirmed without the decision 94 question.

The audit 2026-11-05 #49 fix covers only keys whose every version is Rejected; that case falls back to `versions.Last()`. No plan decision says that a refused correction over a saved line should pass silently. Decision 94's reasoning, that the server does not hold what the review shows, applies here directly.

There is a workaround: running Koosta read again compares the review with the Accepted R and drafts a new M, and the drafts guard would then block the confirmation. But nothing prompts the accountant to do that. The log, the grid and the confirmation all say the month is fine while MTA keeps the old figures. That is a misleading flow, so major. It is not critical, because the app sends no wrong data; it only fails to report that a correction did not land.

**Second skeptic.** I could not refute this, and I checked each point in the code.

1. `SqliteStore.Payments` (SatellesTSD.Infrastructure/SqliteStore.cs:499-506) falls back to `versions.Last()` only when every version of a key is Rejected. For a key that also has an Accepted R, it returns that R. That R belongs to the earlier submission.
2. `ReadFeedback` (PeriodSubmitter.cs:349-350) filters that list by `SubmissionId == submissionId`. The refused M is counted in Recorded but has no status entry. The log line at MainForm.cs:1287 prints only Recorded and the Statuses map, and after it only the general findings. The line's own black message is never logged there.
3. `MarkConfirmed` (PeriodSubmitter.cs:279-293) builds `drafts`, `open` and `refused` from the same `Payments` list. Nothing calls `History` there. So with no rebuild, the month is confirmed without the question.
4. The window (MainForm.cs:1213-1246) adds no check of its own.

Decision 94 says "a refused line is not on the server, so the month confirmed without it is not the month the review shows". Decision 51 as amended by it, and audit 49, are about this same mismatch. No decision (61, 151, 196 included) says a refused M or T over a saved version may pass silently. Decision 151 only covers a later report lifting a refused version, which does not apply here.

The effect: e-MTA keeps the old figures, while the log, the grid and the confirmation all show the month as fine. Only the Ajalugu dialog shows the refused version. Running Koosta read again is a workaround, but nothing prompts the accountant to do it.

No wrong data is sent, so it is not critical. It is a misleading result in a flow the accountant needs, so it stays major.

**Fix:** In MarkConfirmed, also collect keys whose latest version in History(...) is Rejected while an earlier version is on the server (M or T refused over a saved line), and name them in the forceable LINES_PENDING question ('the correction was refused; the server still holds the earlier version'). In ReadFeedback, count statuses over the submission's own versions (History, or a store query by submission_id), not over Payments. Consider flagging such keys in the grid as well.

### 4. [major] A correction (M) or cancellation (T) that MTA refuses black on an accepted line vanishes: the feedback log, the Deklaratsioon tab, the report and Märgi kinnitatuks all show the old line as accepted

flow — `SatellesTSD.Core/Declare/PeriodSubmitter.cs:293`

**Scenario.** October has been declared and accepted. Line 001 is an Accepted R with salary net 1000. The accountant presses Ava parandamiseks and changes the payment to 1200. Koosta read makes an M draft, and the accountant exports and uploads it. MTA refuses the M with a black validation that is not _5 or _6, so RecordFeedback sets the M to Rejected. The Accepted R stays beneath it. (1) ReadFeedback works out its status counts from _store.Payments(...).Where(p => p.SubmissionId == submissionId). Payments (SqliteStore.cs:504) returns the latest non-Rejected version of each key, which is the old R from the earlier submission. So the log says '1 rida;' and names no status: no 'tagasi lükatud'. (2) The Deklaratsioon tab (PeriodDeclarer.Lines -> Payments) shows line 001 green as Vastu võetud, with the R's old feedback, net 1000 and its taxes. The report workbook's Deklaratsioon sheet shows the same. (3) Märgi kinnitatuks: MarkConfirmed's guards read the same Payments list. 'open' holds only Pending/NeedsChange lines. 'refused' holds only standing versions with Status == Rejected, and the Rejected M is never a standing version while an Accepted version exists. So the period is marked Kinnitatud without a question. MTA still holds 1000 while the review and the accountant believe 1200 was declared. A T refused black over an Accepted R ends the same way: a payment the review no longer declares stays on the server, and the app confirms silently.

**Detail.** Decision 94 (audit-2026-11-05 item 49) made the confirmation ask when a key was refused black and so is not on the server. It does not cover a refused later version of a key that is on the server with other figures. Payments() is built to hide Rejected versions behind a standing one, so every view built on it loses the refusal: the feedback summary, the lines grid, DeclarationTotals, the report and MarkConfirmed. FeedbackReport.ActionFor itself returns CorrectWithChange for a black M, so the model knows the server holds the old line. Only the Ajalugu dialog shows the refused version, and the accountant has no reason to open it. The correction is lost without a word, and the month is confirmed with the old figures at MTA.

**Verified.** I traced the code and the claim holds. When a black report comes back on the pending M, RecordFeedback (SqliteStore.cs:697) sets that M to Rejected. The Accepted R underneath it stays as it is. The _5/_6 cascade at line 703 does not apply here, because an M over an existing key gets a different black code. Payments() (SqliteStore.cs:504) then picks LastOrDefault(v => v.Status != Rejected), which is the old Accepted R.

That one choice hides the refusal everywhere the claim says:
(1) ReadFeedback (PeriodSubmitter.cs:349) filters Payments() by p.SubmissionId == this submission. The standing R belongs to the earlier submission, so Statuses comes back empty. MainForm.cs:1287 then logs "1 rida; " and names no status.
(2) PeriodDeclarer.Lines (line 141) takes the R and feeds LastFeedback from _store.Feedback(R.Id), which is the R's old feedback. DeclarationPanel shows the line Honeydew/Accepted with net 1000.
(3) In MarkConfirmed (line 292-293), 'open' looks only at Pending/NeedsChange lines. 'refused' looks only at standing versions whose Status is Rejected, and the Rejected M is never the standing version. With no drafts left, the period is set to Confirmed without any question.

No plan decision covers this case. Decision 94/item 49 covers only keys with no version on the server, and decision 151 is about lifting an earlier refusal. Only the Ajalugu dialog and the submission's overall Result column hint that something went wrong. MTA still holds the old figures, and the app tells the accountant the correction landed. The app itself sends nothing wrong, and e-MTA shows the refusal, so I rate this major (a misleading flow) rather than critical.

**Second skeptic.** The code works as the finding says. When a black outcome comes back on a pending M or T that is not _5 or _6 (or the new-on-existing rule), RecordFeedback sets the M or T to Rejected (SqliteStore.cs:697). The Accepted R underneath it is not touched, because the cascade at 703 runs only for SaysKeyUnknown. Payments() (SqliteStore.cs:504) picks LastOrDefault(Status != Rejected), so it returns the old R. That one choice causes three problems:
(1) ReadFeedback (PeriodSubmitter.cs:349) counts statuses over Payments() filtered to this submission. The R belongs to the earlier submission, so the refused M is left out, and MainForm.cs:1287 logs '1 rida;' with no 'tagasi lükatud'.
(2) The Deklaratsioon lines come from Payments(), so the key shows as the old Accepted R.
(3) In MarkConfirmed (lines 280-293), 'open' and 'refused' look only at standing versions. The Rejected M is never a standing version while the R stands, so the period goes to Confirmed with no question.
No plan decision covers this case. Decision 94 covers keys that have no version on the server, decision 151 covers lifting a refusal, and the S10 notes cover stale drafts and the cascade after a refusal by hand. A refused correction on a key that is on the server is not settled anywhere.
The effect: nothing wrong is sent, and e-MTA and the Ajalugu dialog do show the refusal. But the app's own feedback summary, the lines grid and the confirmation guard all tell the accountant the month is done, while MTA still holds the old figures. That is a misleading flow, so I rate it major, not critical.

**Fix:** In MarkConfirmed, also look at the history of each key: when the latest non-draft version of a key is Rejected (an M or T) and an earlier version is on the server, count it as 'refused, the server holds an earlier version' and ask for the force with the key named. In ReadFeedback, count statuses over every version whose SubmissionId is this submission (from History or a direct query), not over Payments(). In the lines grid, mark a key whose latest sent version was refused, for example by status text or colour, plus the refused version's feedback in the tooltip.

### 5. [minor] Totals cut: a refused hand line holds any type whose line on the server contains another hand line, because every hand line's reference is the same 'by hand'

declare — `SatellesTSD.Core/Declare/PeriodDeclarer.cs:899`

**Scenario.** The month is declared as totals per type. A person has a bank-row salary and a hand line, both type 10. Their totals line L10 ('totals:10:LHV pank *1757 [2]; by hand') is sent and Accepted. The accountant then adds a second hand line (type 17, its date typed in another month, so NOT_IN_PERIOD) and rebuilds. TypesAddingUp(person, 'by hand') splits L10's references on '; ', finds 'by hand', and holds type 10 too. NotePool(L10) records it because L10 used the exemption and the minimum, so the NOT_ALLOCATED branch stops every other payment of the person. The log says 'the line(s) ...-01-001 of a refused payment stand on the server and use the month's exemption or minimum', but L10 does not add up the refused hand line. Without the clash only type 17 would be held, and nothing stands for it, so the person's other payments would be built.

**Detail.** ReviewPayment.Reference is ReviewWorkspace.ByHandSource ('by hand') for every hand line, and TotalsRef keeps payments only by that Reference. So decision 199's 'the line on the server that adds the payment up' cannot tell one hand line from another. The person's whole month is held, and the message points the accountant at a line that has nothing to do with the refusal. Nothing wrong is sent: the server lines stand. The way out is to fix or untick the new hand line.

**Verified.** I could not refute this. The code confirms it. In ReviewView.cs:124, Reference returns ReviewWorkspace.ByHandSource ("by hand") for every hand line. PeriodDeclarer.cs:408 builds the totals source text from those references only (TotalsRef), with no line id. TypesAddingUp (PeriodDeclarer.cs:887-903) splits a standing line's references on "; " and matches the refused payment's Reference by plain string equality. So under the totals cut, any refused hand line matches every totals line on the server that adds up any hand line, and line 349 adds all of those types to refusedTypes.

The held type's line on the server is then kept (lines 395-400). NotePool notes it if it used the exemption or the minimum. Lines 599-603 then raise NOT_ALLOCATED, stop the person's other built payments and name an unrelated line. The trigger is easy to hit: a hand line refused by NOT_IN_PERIOD (ReviewChecks.cs:86) or by any other check.

Decision 199 says a refused payment holds the types whose totals line names it by its reference. It never considers that hand-line references are not unique, so it does not settle this as intended.

The effect leans conservative. Lines on the server stay as they are, so nothing wrong reaches MTA. ## Refuted

- **Pause dates are ignored on a row whose export-time state is no longer 'Peatatud' (pause already over, or contract ended during the pause), so the minimum is charged for paused days** (tor) — The finding depends on a TÖR export that keeps the pause columns filled on a row whose state is Kehtiv (2) or Lõpetatud (4). The plan's record of the real exports rules that out. In docs/SatellesTSD-plan.md, line 488, the decision that added the pause column notes that every paused entry in the real exports (32 of them) has a start date and a reason, and that "no valid or ended entry has any". When a pause ends, the register clears those dates or the row stays in state 3. If it stays in state 3, InForceOn (TorParser.cs:128) and MonthShare (PeriodDeclarer.cs:98, 123-129) already skip the days inside PauseStart..PauseEnd by date, so decision 181 is followed. A state-2 or state-4 row with no pause dates has nothing to skip. A state-4 row's in-force days are bounded by its EndDate, which the register sets when a contract is ended. The finder also admits it could not reproduce the case: the simulator writes pause columns only on state-3 rows, which matches the observed data. The code is consistent with the data the register actually produces, so this scenario does not occur. At most it would be a defensive hardening.
- **A register employee's salary row that the name search misses keeps the codeless 'TÖR-väline palgad' category when the accountant gives it to the employee: it is never declared, never counted, and only a minimum zero line is built** (matching) — The trace is right, but the app works this way on purpose and says so on screen. ReviewWorkspace.SetPerson changes only the person. SetCategory is a separate edit, and the plan's tests list "a row nobody matched given to a person and a category" as the intended flow. Decision 41 says the same: the accountant gives the row a person with the Isik combo and a category with its own combo. Decision 27 sends rows the search misses or drops (AMBIGUOUS_MASK) to the second pass under 'TÖR-väline palgad' on purpose. That copies the workbook, where the accountant fixed such rows by hand. Decision 10 makes that category codeless and never declared. The outcome is not hidden. In the payments grid (MainForm.cs 2306-2355), the row the accountant just gave to the employee shows the category 'TÖR-väline palgad' ("salary outside TÖR", plainly wrong for a register employee) right beside the person combo. It shows NO_TYPE in the check column with a tooltip, and it is greyed as not declared. The persons grid shows PALK NETO KOKKU as 0 for that employee. The build then names the person with ZERO_LINE ("no pay carrying the minimum this month"). So the person-level signal the finder says is missing is there. The fix is one combo choice: picking Palgad derives type 10, and the row is declared by default. The trigger is also narrow: an LHV export usually keeps diacritics, and the AMBIGUOUS_MASK drops are already listed as problems. Re-deriving the category in the employee pass when a person is given would be a nice improvement, at most minor, but it is not a defect that silently sends wrong data.
- **A salary worded 'tasu' with a day range ('Töötasu 10-2026', 'Lisatasu 1-15 okt') also matches the address patterns of Üür and Kommunaalkulud: it becomes ambiguous, is not declared or counted, and a minimum zero line is built** (matching) — I'm refuting this. The match does happen as described, but the app already treats it as a case for the accountant to decide, so nothing is declared without warning.

1. **The match is real.** "Töötasu 10-2026" contains "su 10-2", which fits the Üür and Kommunaalkulud mask `*[a-z][a-z] [0-9][0-9]-[0-9]*`. The only salary word those two cells exclude is `*palk*`, so Categorizer.cs:85-102 returns an Ambiguous verdict. The finder's result file agrees: Category null, Declare false, and a type 10 zero line.

2. **The masks are meant to work this way.** They come unchanged from the old workbook. Decision 25 checked them against the workbook's own reports ("tn 24-51" and "vi 29-15" match, "Linnamäe 1-7" does not). The exclude lists are the owner's standard filter set, not new code.

3. **The app does not hide the row.**
   - The categorizer writes the workbook's own error note on it ("viga: kategooria tuvastamine [Palgad] [Üür] [Kommunaalkulud]").
   - It sets ReviewPayment.ConflictingCategories (ReviewWorkspace.cs:929), so the row is listed in the "Kategooriate konflikt" tab (decision 148; MainForm.cs:2110). That tab is there for exactly this: the accountant picks the category, and the pick then gets the right type code and is declared.
   - Its category is null, so it is also listed in "Tundmatu kategooria" (MainForm.cs:2109).

4. **The owner can fix the wording.** Version 0.34.0 gave the owner a phrases file to change the catalog. Adding `*tasu*` or `*haigus*` to the exclude lists is a tuning request, not a fault in the code.

The weak point is real: an undecided conflict leaves only a zero line with the minimum, and Deklareerimata stays silent. But the row is flagged in two tabs and carries an error note, so there is a clear workaround. At most this would be a minor suggestion to improve the catalog.
- **A register person whose codeless row comes before their coded row is split into two persons, so every exact hit becomes a SHARED_ROW counted for nobody** (matching) — I traced the code, and the split it describes does happen: a coded row that comes after a codeless row of the same names gets its own key in MatchModel.cs:69-71. But this is deliberate behaviour with a test pinning it, not an oversight. SatellesTSD.Tests/PeriodMatcherTests.cs:113-114 builds exactly this register (KASK MARI codeless twice, then coded "1"), expects the persons ["KASK|MARI", "1"], and comments that "a coded row after a codeless one of the same names was a second person to the workbook". The FromTor doc comment says the same: only a codeless row joins an earlier namesake, as the workbook's IsDuplicateEntry did. So the rule copies the workbook it replaces, on purpose. The scenario is also unlikely in a real export. A TÖR export from the tax board shows the person's current identity on all their rows, so a codeless row next to a coded one of the same names would mean a hand-edited copy. Decision 168 / audit item 21 cover that case: the export says how many rows lack a code, and a codeless register person is reported as skipped. Nothing wrong is declared either. The hits become SHARED_ROW, counted for nobody, with a visible problem message, and the accountant can give the rows to the coded person. At worst it is a visible prompt with a workaround, on a path the code chose on purpose.
- **"Märgi mitteresidentidele maksuvaba avaldus" also ticks the exemption for non-resident rent payees, which wipes out the income tax on their rent** (review) — I traced the code and it works as the finder says. NonResidentsShown (ReviewWorkspace.cs:737-741) takes every person the period shows whose residency is NonResident, and GiveExemptionToNonResidents ticks every one of them that is inside the EEA. The persons shown include payee persons made under decision 108.

That scope is the settled design, though. Decision 201 (docs/SatellesTSD-plan.md:731) says the command "ticks the application for every non-resident the month shows without it, after one question that names how many". It also mirrors the removal command ClearExemptionOfNonResidents, which uses the same NonResidentsShown set (decision 173), so the two clicks undo each other. That symmetry would break if the tick reached only register persons.

The flow does not change anything silently:
- The question gives the exact count. In the scenario it says 3 when the accountant expects 2 employees.
- The accountant must say yes.
- The log names each person ticked.
- The tick can be undone with the removal command or on the card.

The law does not forbid it either. A non-resident person paid rent can have the basic exemption applied by the payer if they file the application, and the engine marks type 205 as BasicExemptionAllowed. So the result is not data that is wrong by rule. It follows from a bulk command whose stated scope is every non-resident the month shows. At most this is a minor wish: name the persons in the question, or keep payee persons out. It is not a defect against the plan.
- **Any residence country not written as an ISO alpha-2 code ("Latvia", "Läti", "LVA") counts as outside the EEA: payments are refused EXEMPTION_NOT_EEA and the new bulk tick skips the person** (review) — The scenario can't happen through the card. The only real input for a person's ResidenceCountry is PersonPanel.Save at PersonPanel.cs:308, which reads `_country`. That box is declared at PersonPanel.cs:31 as `new TextBox { CharacterCasing = CharacterCasing.Upper, MaxLength = 2 }`, so the accountant can't type "Latvia", "Läti" or "LVA" on the card. Whatever they type becomes at most two capital letters. No other code path sets a stored person's ResidenceCountry. That leaves SqliteStore Clean, test fixtures, and the Simulator/Simulation types. A long country name only gets in through the simulator's JSON or SimulationDialog's own box, which has no MaxLength. Simulation runs are what-if tools (decision 204), and their output is never the declaration sent to MTA. So the claimed refusal, the bulk tick's left-out note, and `<accountSubID>Latvia</accountSubID>` are artifacts of the simulator input only. What remains is a two-letter text that is not an ISO code (e.g. "XX"). It would be called "outside the EEA", and the refusal shows the typed value so the accountant can see and fix it. Decision 102/144 already treat the field as optional, with MTA's KR_ISIK_6 register check as the net. At most this is a nit: the simulator dialog could cap its country box at two letters like the card does.
- **A page with a withholding start later than the end of contributions (a person who rejoined the II pillar) reads as 'not a member' for good** (lookups) — The code does what the finding says. MemberOn (LookupModel.cs:60) applies ContributionsEnded whatever the order of the dates, and PensionikeskusParser keeps the first date of each kind (`ended ??= date`). But plan decision 35 sets this rule on purpose: "a member = the withholding begun by the control date and the contributions not ended before it (the day they ended is no member's day, the workbook's rule)", and both lines are read when the page carries both.

The page in the finding is invented. None of the delivered real page shapes and none of the test fixtures shows an end line next to a later withholding start. The finding itself says the case is latent: under the post-2021 exit rules a person can rejoin only after ten years, so no rejoin is possible before about 2031, well past the 2026 rates, the only year the app supports. Nothing shows that Pensionikeskus would still print the old "sissemaksed lõppenud" line after a rejoin, rather than only the new withholding start.

The scenario cannot happen with current law and known page shapes, and the behaviour is the documented rule, so I refute it. At most it is a hardening nit for a later page format.
- **Vahearuanne 'Töötamise lõpu aeg' ignores decision 80: shows a running fixed-term contract's future end, and nothing in a gap month** (reports) — The code does what the finder says: ReportBuilder.Interim:93/101 writes register.Entry's raw EndDate, while MainForm.EmploymentEndValue (MainForm.cs:3332) applies decision 80's rule. But no plan decision tells the report to use that rule. Decision 80 (plan line 625) and item 4 (line 478) are about the persons grid column, which the owner asked for on 2026-11-05. The Vahearuanne's "Töötamise lõpu aeg" is older. It came with S9 as the macro's workbook column (plan line 32, line 367: "as the workbook the accountants know"), and that column carries the register's end date. Decision 121 makes the report follow the grid only for the columns it lists (Peatamise algus, Peatamise lõpu aeg, Deklareerimine, Deklareerimata). Decision 99 is about the grid too. So the difference is how the report was built on purpose, not a broken rule.

Case 1: the report shows the TÖR register's real end date for a fixed-term contract. That is true register data, and it is a future date in a monthly report, so it hardly reads as "ended". Case 2: the report's row stays consistent with itself. Start, type, Kande olek and end all come from the same entry (register.Entry, the December contract), which is decision 99's "five columns read as one employment".

Nothing here goes to the tax board, and the declaration is not affected. At most this is a cosmetic difference between the grid and the workbook, and the owner could choose to align them (nit). Refuted.
- **A basic-exemption tick that a register answer took off is never put back when a later answer, for another month or for the corrected code, says resident; the build then declares a resident employee with no exemption and says nothing** (integrity) — The plan decides this behaviour on purpose. LookupRunner.cs:317 takes the tick off only when an answer makes the person a non-resident, and decision 173 then says: "Nothing puts the tick back when a person becomes a resident again: that is the accountant's to say." That covers Case A. The card is shared by every month and its facts are present-tense, so from the card's side Case A is a person who was made a non-resident and then made a resident again. Decision 173 hands the tick back to the accountant in exactly that case. The run does not hide the removal either: it reports the step "basic exemption application taken off: a non-resident". Case B is covered by decision 180, which lists only the residency, the pension and the control date as what goes when the code is corrected, and adds: "A fact set by hand stands, and so does every other field of the card". So keeping BasicExemptionApplied as it stands is that decision as written. Case B also needs a mistyped code that is valid and belongs to a real non-resident. The removal is shown in the run's steps and the tick is visible on the card. Re-declaring a reopened month marks the changed lines, so the accountant sees the higher income tax before exporting. Putting the tick back automatically is a design change the plan has already turned down, not a defect. The proposed note (EXEMPTION_TAKEN_BY_REGISTER) would be a useful extra, nothing more.
