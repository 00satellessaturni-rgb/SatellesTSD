# SatellesTSD — fourth audit, the whole app (2026-11-17, version 0.32.0, commit e77037f)

**How it was made.** Fourteen Opus finders, each on one area of the whole app, read-only and told to skip what the three earlier audits record as open, refused or settled by decision (and to report anything marked FIXED that is not): reading files; the TÖR export and contracts; matching; the review; tax computation; declaring; the XML out and the feedback in; the register lookups; the store and its migrations; the reports; the self-update; the window; and two cross-cutting sweeps (the monthly flow end to end, and data integrity across months and companies). Every finding then went to a skeptic told to refute it by tracing the code; a critical or major one went to a second skeptic as well, told to refute it by the plan's decisions and its real effect. 29 findings: 24 confirmed by every skeptic, 2 split (one skeptic refuted them, the other did not — kept, and the refutation quoted), 3 refuted. The severity below is the highest one a confirming skeptic gave; the finder's own is lowered where a skeptic lowered it. No critical survived (the one critical, a corrected personal code, was rated major by both skeptics).

Severity: critical = wrong tax or declaration data sent to the tax board, or data loss; major = a flow the accountant needs that breaks or misleads; minor = a wrong message or an edge case with a workaround; nit = cosmetic.

## The short list — the majors

1. **Pay to a store person the month's TÖR does not list gets a full social-tax minimum top-up** (item 1). A final settlement in the month after a contract ended, given to the person by hand, is declared with the minimum's whole top-up, while the same pay to a person still listed with an ended contract gets none.
2. **"Eemalda fail" in a reopened month deletes the file behind accepted totals lines** (item 2). The file is unlinked first, so the store's guard, which looks for totals lines through the link, finds none.
3. **An update counts as a success even when the new version cannot open the store** (item 3). The new copy shows the error and still signals ready, so the old copy (which could open the store) hands over and is replaced, with no automatic way back.
4. **A card export's reverted, declined or failed rows are read as payments** (item 4, split). The State column is never read; a reverted transfer and its resend, both completed in the month, are both declared. One skeptic doubts the export fills the completion date of a reverted row.
5. **A personal code corrected on the card keeps the registers' answers for the old code** (item 5). Residency, pension and the check dates stay, and the build declares with them without a word.
6. **The zero line is decided by the contract's state on the export day** (item 6). A contract that covered the whole month but was paused or ended after it (as the TÖR export downloaded the next month shows it) loses that month's minimum line.

The minors (items 7–25) and the nit (26) follow, most severe first. Two of them were seen before and left: item 17 (unlinking every file keeps the drafts) was refuted as out of scope in the review of 2026-11-17 and is real; item 24 (the totals cut and a refused payment) was split.

## The findings

### 1. [major] Pay to a store person whom the month's TÖR does not list gets a full social-tax minimum top-up; the same pay to a person listed with an ended contract gets none

flow — `SatellesTSD.Core/Declare/PeriodDeclarer.cs:524`

**Scenario.** An employee's Tööleping ended on 31.10. The November TÖR export no longer lists them, but the store person (TorEmploymentTypeId 1, SocialTaxMinimumApplies true) exists from October. A final settlement is paid on 05.11 (net 200). The search finds the row for nobody, since only register persons are searched, and the accountant gives it to the person. FromHit derives type 10 from the card's TÖR type (ReviewDefaults.EmploymentTypeFor with register null) and ticks it to declare. In BuildPerson, reviewPerson.Register is null, so entry is null and inRegister is false. MonthShare(null, ...) returns 1 (PeriodDeclarer.cs:91-94). The NO_EMPLOYMENT_IN_MONTH branch at line 524 runs only when `inRegister && entry is null`, so the minimum stays on. MonthAllocator then tops the line up with TSD_KUUMSUURENDUS to the full 886 (about 626 of increase, roughly 206 EUR of extra social tax). No note is written: only the log line 'SM miinimum 886,00 (osa 1), puudujääk …'.

**Detail.** The code already decides that pay after the employment ended carries no minimum. It applies that rule only when the export still lists the ended row (inRegister && entry is null). A month export that leaves out a contract ended in an earlier month, which is the usual shape of a month's TÖR query, hits the other path. There, an unlisted person paid under an employee type is treated as employed for the whole month (share 1) and the minimum is topped up to the full kuumäär. So the same final settlement is declared with or without a social-tax minimum depending only on whether the export happens to list the ended row, and the version with the top-up is wrong SM data in the XML. Nothing in the review or the build marks the difference. The workaround (untick the minimum on the card) also switches it off for later months, by decision 81.

**Verified.** I traced the path and it holds. ReviewWorkspace.cs:332 builds store persons who have lines but are not in the month's register with `Make(null, person, null, ...)`, so `reviewPerson.Register` is null. In FromHit (ReviewWorkspace.cs:759), `ReviewDefaults.EmploymentTypeFor(null, person, day)` falls back to `person.TorEmploymentTypeId`. That is decision 40, so a former Tööleping person still gets salary type 10, and the payment is declared by default. In PeriodDeclarer.cs:412-413, `entry` is null and `inRegister` is false. `MonthShare(null, ...)` returns 1 (lines 91-94). The only switch-off for pay after the employment ended is at line 527, and it requires `inRegister && entry is null`. The `share == 0` branch cannot run either, because the share is 1. So the facts keep `SocialTaxMinimumApplies` from the card, and the allocator tops the line up to the full kuumäär with no NO_EMPLOYMENT_IN_MONTH note.

This goes against the plan's own rule. Decision 48 says pay in a month the person's register rows do not cover owes no minimum. The owner's item 5 and decision 137 say only "kaasa" treats a person with no register row and a payment as validly employed. Under "reeglite järgi" (the default), that person should not get the minimum. No decision chooses a full-month minimum for an unlisted person on purpose. No earlier audit records it either: the 2026-11-10 refuted item is about a "kaasa" zero line, which is a different case.

The scenario is realistic. A month's TÖR export leaves out a contract that ended in an earlier month, and the final settlement or the previous month's salary is paid in the new month. The accountant assigns the unmatched row to the existing person. The declared social tax is then wrong, and nothing in the build says so.

I rate it major rather than critical. It needs the accountant to give the row to the person by hand, and the topped-up social-tax figure is visible on the line before export. Still, it can send wrong social-tax data to the tax board without any warning.

**Fix:** For a person with payments but no register row in the month's run (reviewPerson.Register is null), and not 'kaasa', treat the minimum like the ended-employment case: switch it off and note NO_EMPLOYMENT_IN_MONTH. If such pay should carry the minimum after all, at least add a build note naming the top-up for a person the register does not list, so the accountant decides.

### 2. [major] Eemalda fail in a reopened month defeats the store's totals guard: the file behind accepted totals lines is deleted

store — `SatellesTSD.Core/Review/PeriodPipeline.cs:319`

**Scenario.** A month uses the totals cut ('totals per liik'). It is exported and accepted, so its totals lines are Accepted, then reopened with 'Ava parandamiseks' and is a Draft again. In Failid the accountant picks 'Eemalda fail' on the bank file whose rows those totals add up, and gives the second word to clear the run. RemoveFile runs inside one transaction: UnlinkImport (PeriodPipeline.cs:319) deletes the period_import row first, then RemoveImport runs (:323). RemoveImport's totals guard (SqliteStore.Imports.cs:390-400) only finds totals lines through period_import, and that link was just deleted in the same transaction, so it counts 0. The sentPeriods guard (:402-408) passes because the month is a Draft. The declared guard (:368-379) only sees lines that name a row, and totals lines name none. So the file, its sheets and its rows leave the store, although lines built from them stand on the server. The same happens in two steps: 'Eemalda perioodist' first, then 'Eemalda fail' on the now unlisted file. The per-payment cut of the same month is refused (declared guard) and the whole transaction rolls back, so only the totals cut loses its source.

**Detail.** Decision 132 says a file is taken out 'with the store's own refusal for a line on the server', and RemoveImport has a dedicated totals check for this ('declared line(s) on the server add up this file's rows among others; it stays as their source'). Because the unlink runs before the guard in the same transaction, the check can never fire in the flow the window uses. A re-read (ReplaceImport) keeps the link while it checks, so there the same guard works. What is lost is the stored file and its reading: the record behind a declaration already on the server, which the store promises to keep.

**Verified.** The finding holds. I traced it in the code. Reopen (PeriodSubmitter.cs:311-320) only sets the period back to Draft, so its totals lines keep their Sent or Accepted status. RemoveFile (PeriodPipeline.cs:293-325) passes RequireDraft because the month is a Draft again. No other period lists the file, so the checks at :299-307 pass too. Inside one InTransaction, the store's nested Tx() runs on the same connection, so a deletion is visible to the queries that follow. UnlinkImport (SqliteStore.Imports.cs:337-360) only refuses a period past Draft, or a run that still holds the file, and the run is cleared first when the accountant agrees to clear it. It then deletes the period_import row (:358). RemoveImport's guards then run:
- The declared check (:368-379) joins on source_row_id, and totals lines have none.
- The draft check (:381-387) also needs a row id.
- The totals check (:390-400) joins period_import, whose row was just deleted, so it counts 0.
- The sentPeriods check (:402-408) counts only months past Draft.
- RunsUsing and DecisionsOn are cleared by the run clear and by the unlink.
So the file, its sheets and its rows are deleted even though totals lines built from them are on the server. Taking the file out of the period first (UnlinkFile :430-434, which has no on-server check) and then choosing "Eemalda fail" from the unlisted files gives the same result. The guard does work in the re-read path, which keeps the link while it checks. I rate it major, not critical. Nothing wrong reaches the tax board and the declared lines stay. What goes is the stored source record behind them, and only after the accountant's explicit two-step confirmation; re-importing the file restores it.

**Fix:** In RemoveFile, run RemoveImport's on-server checks (or a store method that checks without the link, e.g. totals lines of any period that lists or listed the file, by its rows' dates and persons) before UnlinkImport. Alternatively, pass the period being unlinked to RemoveImport so its totals check still counts that period's on-server totals lines. UnlinkImport could also refuse in a month that holds on-server totals lines from the run that read the file.

### 3. [major] The update counts as a success even when the new version cannot open the store, so the old version that still could is replaced with no automatic way back

update — `SatellesTSD/MainForm.cs:594`

**Scenario.** A release whose schema migration fails on this office's real store. For example, a new unique index that the existing rows break, or an I/O error during the upgrade transaction. The accountant clicks Uuenda ja käivita uuesti. The new copy starts with --update-ready. In OnLoad it calls OpenStore(path), and new AppServices(path) throws from EnsureSchema. The transaction rolls back, so the store is still at the old schema. Fail(e) shows the error box, the label reads 'no store', and the accountant clicks OK. The posted CheckForUpdatesAsync then runs and calls StartupFinished(result.State != Failed), which signals ready. The old window's WaitFor returns Ready, HandOver sets Started, and the old window closes. Result: the accountant is left in a new version that cannot open the store, the old version is gone from the program folder, failed.txt is not written, and nothing offers to put the previous version back. The only way back is the manual copy from .SatellesTSD-update\previous-<stamp>. The old version would have opened the untouched store without trouble.

**Detail.** The start-up handshake (Program.ReadySignal, MainForm.StartupFinished at MainForm.cs:594 and :644-658) only means 'the window is up and the first check ran'. It never asks whether the store the update handed over could be opened. OpenStore (MainForm.cs:1371-1421) catches the failure and shows it, so it never reaches the ThreadException handler that would end the copy with FailedExitCode (Program.cs:66-75). The old copy only puts itself back on Exited or on the accountant's 'Taasta eelmine versioon' after a silent minute (UpdateDialog.cs:283-295). So the failure the store copy and decision 130 were built for (an upgrade that fails) becomes a successful hand-over, but only when the accountant answers the error box within StartTimeoutMs. If they wait longer, the old copy shows the no-answer choice instead. Decision 66 describes the ready signal but says nothing about a store that does not open, so this outcome is not chosen on purpose.

**Verified.** I traced it and the path is real. OnLoad (MainForm.cs:1215-1232) calls OpenStore(path) when the store exists. If new AppServices throws from a migration that is not IsNewerThanThisBuild and no restore is offered, the catch at MainForm.cs:1390-1421 only calls Fail(e), sets the label to "no store" and returns, so nothing reaches Program's ThreadException/FailedExitCode handler. OnLoad then posts CheckForUpdatesAsync. It reaches StartupFinished(result.State != Failed) at MainForm.cs:594, and that only asks whether the release-folder check worked, so it calls Updater.SignalReady. In the old copy, UpdateDialog.AwaitAnswerAsync (UpdateDialog.cs:282-287) gets Ready and calls HandOver. The old window then closes with _handedOver set (MainForm.cs:720-726). The previous version stays aside, nothing is written to failed.txt and the app offers no way back. Decisions 130 and 156 make the store's upgrade transactional and offer the store copy back only when the new copy ends. Decision 157 is about a new copy left running, not about one that runs but cannot open its store. No decision chooses this outcome. The real impact is narrow, though. It needs a migration that fails on a real store (a rare release defect or a lock or I/O error). The error box has to be answered within the one-minute wait, otherwise the old copy offers "Taasta eelmine versioon". The store itself is untouched and no data is lost. The workarounds are to copy the program back from the hidden previous-<stamp> folder or to publish a fixed release. So it is an edge case with a workaround, not a broken everyday flow.

**Fix:** While Program.ReadySignal is set, treat a failed OpenStore in OnLoad as a failed start when a store was named (the remembered one or the one handed on) and it exists, so not for 'store missing', which opens a dialog. Log the failure to viga.log and call Environment.Exit(Updater.FailedExitCode) instead of only showing the error. Do this at least for errors that are not IsNewerThanThisBuild. The old copy then reverts, notes the build in failed.txt and takes the store up again.

### 4. [major] Card/e-money export (source1): reverted, declined and failed rows are read as payments because the State column is never read (one skeptic refuted it)

import — `SatellesTSD.Core/Import/layouts.json:211`

**Scenario.** A source1 (Revolut-shape) export for October has a salary transfer of -905.92 to an employee with State REVERTED (the bank sent it back). The accountant then resent it, and that row has State COMPLETED. Both rows carry a 'Date completed (UTC)' in October and the same amount. They have different IDs, so PeriodMatcher's duplicate check (trusted ids differ) does not merge them. Both rows are read as debit payments to the person, and the person's October payout is summed and declared as 1811.84 instead of 905.92.

**Detail.** The source1 layout lists 'State' among its headers, but LayoutRoles has no role for it. StatementParser.Parse (StatementParser.cs:404-423) turns every row that names a counterparty into a payment row, whatever its state. Direction comes from the sign alone (line 373), and the month filter looks only at the date (PeriodMatcher.cs:269). No category filter in matching.json looks at the state either: source1 uses 'standard'. A row whose money never moved (REVERTED, DECLINED, FAILED) but that carries a completion date and an amount therefore reaches the sums and the declaration lines. The grid does not show the State cell, so the accountant has no sign of it. A PENDING or DECLINED row with no completion date drops out only by luck: its date is null, so it is not in the month. This assumes the export fills 'Date completed (UTC)' for a reverted row, as the bank's API does with completed_at for a transaction that completed before it was reverted.

**Verified.** I could not refute it. Most of it is confirmed from the code. One part depends on the data and can't be checked here. The source1 layout (SatellesTSD.Core/Import/layouts.json:204-213) lists "State" only as a header. Its Roles map Account, Date ("Date completed (UTC)"), Counterparty ("Description"), Amount, Description, Currency and TransactionId, and nothing in SatellesTSD.Core reads a statement State or Type cell. Grepping for State or REVERTED finds only the TÖR "Kande olek" and the period states. StatementParser.Parse (StatementParser.cs:331-423) skips a row only when it has no counterparty (SkipReason.NoName). Every other row becomes a payment StatementRow, and for DirectionRule.Sign the direction comes from the sign alone (line 373). The plan's decisions 16 and 17 and the audits never mention card-export row states, so this is not a decision and not an earlier finding. The audit-2026-11-10 item 4 on source1 is about the Amount column, a different issue. So a row that names a counterparty, has a negative Amount and has an in-month completion date is read as a debit payout to the matched person, whatever its State. The grid gives no sign of it. Two things limit the severity. First, the scenario assumes the export fills "Date completed (UTC)" for a REVERTED or FAILED row. If the cell is blank, line 351 records a BadDate problem on the named row, so the accountant sees it. Second, the accountant can untick Deklareeri on the extra row. So this is a silent wrong payout that depends on the export's shape, with a workaround. I rate it major (it misleads in a payroll flow), not critical.

**The other skeptic.** The code part is accurate. The source1 layout (layouts.json:205-213) gives "State" no role, and StatementParser.Parse (StatementParser.cs:371-423) makes every named row a payment, with its direction taken from the sign alone. No plan decision or earlier audit records this. But the harm depends on something the code cannot show and the finding itself only assumes: that the export fills "Date completed (UTC)" for a REVERTED, DECLINED or FAILED row. In card and e-money exports of this shape, a declined, failed or pending row, and a card authorisation that was reverted, normally has no completion date. Such a row has a null Date, so it falls outside every month and never reaches the sums. A transfer the beneficiary's bank sends back usually stays COMPLETED with a separate incoming row, and decision 141 already refuses money in as a payout. That leaves only the case where a row completed and was then marked REVERTED in place, and its completion date is still filled. Nothing in the repo, the plan or the reference material shows this happens. Even then the accountant would see it: the review lists both rows for the same person, same amount and same month, each with its own ID and description, and either can be unticked before the export. The behaviour also matches the workbook the app replaces (CONFIG row 24 lists State only as a header and the workbook never read it), and the owner's real reports were reproduced on it. I cannot confirm wrong data reaching the declaration from the code, so I count it as refuted. If such an export does turn up, it would be at most a minor edge case with a workaround (untick the row), not a major one.

**Fix:** Give the layout a state role (for example LayoutRoles.State plus a list of accepted values such as COMPLETED). In Parse, mark a row whose state is not accepted as skipped, with a new SkipReason, so it is listed but never offered as a payment. At the least, flag it as a problem on the row.

### 5. [major] A personal code corrected on the card keeps the residency and pension the registers gave for the old code, and the build declares with them without a word

integrity — `SatellesTSD.Core/Review/ReviewWorkspace.cs:691`

**Scenario.** A person is looked up for a month (Kontrolli registrid) under code X. X is a typo: EMTA's "not found" reads as resident, and Pensionikeskus answers for X, for example "not joined", or another real person's membership and rate. The person card then shows ResidencySource Emta, PensionSource Pensionikeskus, FactsControlDate in the month and the dates of the check. The accountant corrects the code on the card to Y, the person's real code, and saves without running Päringud again. SavePerson writes PersonalCode = Y but leaves Residency, FundedPensionMember, PensionRate, the sources, the checked-on dates and FactsControlDate as they were. The review finds nothing to refuse: ResidencyCheckedOn is set and the pension is known. 'Koosta read' builds the person's lines under Y with X's facts, with no FACTS_* note, because the control day still matches. The file then goes to MTA with the wrong TSD_KP: none for a real II-pillar member, or someone else's rate. If the real person is a non-resident, it also carries the wrong residency (resident types, 22 % with the 700 exemption).

**Detail.** SavePerson (ReviewWorkspace.cs:687-733) sets residencyChanged only from the residency fields (:691-692) and pensionChanged only from the pension fields (:695). A changed PersonalCode resets neither the facts nor their source, checked-on dates or FactsControlDate. Decision 33 makes the lookup runner ask afresh for a corrected code (LookupRunner.RecordedOrAskedAsync:429 compares recorded.PersonalCode == code), but only when the accountant runs a lookup again. Nothing makes that happen. The build's freshness notes (PeriodDeclarer.cs:499-519) compare only the control date with the month and the person's day, never the code the facts were asked for. ReviewChecks.Of (ReviewChecks.cs:73-76) only asks that ResidencyCheckedOn be non-null. So facts that belong to another code pass as checked. RegisterPersons.Ensure (:62-71) adopting a register code for a codeless person cannot hit this, because a codeless person was never looked up. The card's correction is the path that does.

**Verified.** I confirmed this from the code. In ReviewWorkspace.cs:687-718, SavePerson takes the new PersonalCode from the card through CheckedCode. It works out residencyChanged (:691-692) and pensionChanged (:695) only from the residency, country, member and rate fields. Because `next = fresh with {...}`, it keeps fresh.ResidencySource/PensionSource (Emta/Pensionikeskus), the checked-on dates and FactsControlDate whenever only the code changed. Both callers (MainForm.cs:2087 and :2992) pass the card's edit straight through, and nothing on the window side resets the facts or starts a lookup when the code changes.

Nothing downstream catches it either. PeriodDeclarer.cs:500-519 compares only FactsControlDate with the month and the control day. ReviewChecks only asks that a checked-on date exists, and its code check (:34-35) only tests the code's structure. Decision 33's "a corrected code is asked about afresh" (LookupRunner) applies only when Päringud runs again.

So this sequence is real: a typo code X that still passes the structure check is looked up for the month, then corrected on the card to Y. The build then declares Y with X's pension facts, and residency facts, and shows no note.

Decision 142's way out through the card covers codes that fail the structure check. Those are never looked up (PersonPanel.cs:253 needs LooksValid), so it does not cover this case. No numbered decision chooses to keep register facts across a code change.

I rate it major rather than critical. It needs a typo that passes the structure check and still gets a register answer, and the accountant must then skip a re-run of Päringud after correcting the code. Running Päringud again repairs it, because the runner asks afresh for the new code.

**Fix:** In SavePerson, when the saved code differs from fresh.PersonalCode, drop the register-sourced facts: for a ResidencySource of Emta and a PensionSource of Pensionikeskus or Derived, set ResidencyCheckedOn/PensionCheckedOn and FundedPensionMember/PensionRate to null, the sources to Unknown, and FactsControlDate to null. The review then refuses with RESIDENCY_NOT_CHECKED or PENSION_NOT_CHECKED until Päringud runs for the new code. Leave facts the accountant set by hand alone. Optionally add a build note that compares the latest lookup record's PersonalCode with the person's current code.

### 6. [major] The zero line is decided by the contract's state on the export day, not by its dates in the declared month, so a contract paused or ended after the month loses that month's minimum

tor — `SatellesTSD.Core/Declare/PeriodDeclarer.cs:422`

**Scenario.** The accountant declares October 2026 in early November and downloads the TÖR export then. One employee has a Tööleping (type 1) that ran all of October and was ended on 3 November, or paused (parental leave) from 1 November. In the export that row is kande olek 4 (with EndDate 03.11.2026) or 3 (with PauseStart 01.11.2026). The employee has no minimum-carrying pay dated in October, for example an unpaid month or salary paid only after the month. FromTor still picks the row as ActiveEntry, because Overlaps is date-based. MonthShare also gives a share of 1, because the dates cover all of October and the pause starts after it. But the zero-line test checks the export-time state. At line 422, `entry is { StateId: TorStates.Valid }` fails. Line 423 finds no other Valid carrier and falls back to the same entry. At line 424, `carrier is { StateId: TorStates.Valid }` fails, and without 'kaasa' zeroTypeId is null. So no zero line is built, and October's TSD goes out without the 886 EUR social-tax minimum the employment owed. The correction case is worse. Say September was declared with its zero line while the row was Kehtiv. The period is later reopened and searched against a newer export in which the contract has since ended. The build no longer claims the minimum key, and Retire drafts a T that cancels the accepted zero line. That is a wrong cancellation sent to MTA.

**Detail.** Decision 48 limits the zero line to 'a valid register entry'. The code reads 'valid' as the row's current Kande oleku ID, but a TÖR export is a snapshot taken when it is downloaded (decision 21), usually after the declared month. Every other month-dependent rule here goes by dates: Overlaps, MonthShare's day marking with PauseStart/PauseEnd, and NO_EMPLOYMENT_IN_MONTH. Only the zero-line carrier (lines 422-426, and the INCLUDED message at 435/440) goes by the state, so the two can disagree for the same row. A month whose share is 1 can still get no zero line. The same snapshot effect is in MatchPerson.FromTor and EntryOn (MatchModel.cs:50, :87). They put 'a valid one first', so an export taken after a later change can pick a different standing entry or salary type than the one that held during the month. In the zero-line path this decides whether the minimum is declared at all. This case is not in audits 2026-11-05/09/10: item 23 of 11-05 changed which row carries the minimum, not the state test.

**Verified.** The code path is as the finding describes. MatchPerson.FromTor (MatchModel.cs:86-89) chooses ActiveEntry by date overlap, and an ended or paused row whose dates cover the month qualifies. MonthShare (PeriodDeclarer.cs:119-130) also goes by dates. It marks an Ended row by its Start/End span. It marks a Paused row by everything outside PauseStart..PauseEnd, so a pause that starts after the month, or an end date after the month, gives a share of 1. The zero-line carrier, though, tests the export-time state. At PeriodDeclarer.cs:422-424, `entry is { StateId: TorStates.Valid }` and the Valid filter on line 423 both fail for kande olek 3 or 4. The fallback `?? entry` returns the same non-valid row, so zeroTypeId is null unless the card is set to Include (line 425). No zero line is built. Nothing reports it either: the INCLUDED message (440) applies only under Include, and the NO_EMPLOYMENT_IN_MONTH notes (527-541) apply only when there is pay. Decision 48 does say 'valid register entry', but decision 89 states that a paused row counts its days outside the pause for the minimum. So the code is not following a deliberate choice: the same row owes the full minimum when it has pay and owes nothing without pay. A TÖR export is a snapshot (decision 21) and is normally taken after the month. The scenario is therefore realistic: a contract ended or paused early in the next month, with no minimum-carrying pay in the declared month, means the TSD is missing the social-tax minimum and nobody is told. The workaround is the card's 'kaasa' choice, but the accountant has no signal to use it. The retire-on-rebuild variant needs a period re-searched against a newer export, which is narrower. I rate it major rather than critical because the trigger is narrow (no minimum-carrying pay in an employed month) and the grid does show the row's state.

**Fix:** Decide a row's validity for the month from its dates rather than its export-time state. Treat a row as valid for the month when it covers days of the month outside its pause (a Paused row whose PauseStart falls after the month's last day, or an Ended row whose EndDate is on or after the month's first day, counts as valid for that month). Take the zero-line carrier from those rows. Simplest form: build the zero line whenever MonthShare's carrier rows cover at least one unpaused day of the month, with the type taken from such a row. Apply the same date-based validity to the 'valid first' ordering in FromTor/EntryOn.

**Detail.** bySource can hold a dead or all-refused key for a source, because Liveness ranks Rejected and Cancelled keys below live ones but still keeps them. **Verified.** The finding holds. I traced it as follows. SqliteStore.Payments (SqliteStore.cs:504) returns one row per key: the latest version that is not Rejected, or else the last version. In bySource it ranks at Liveness 2 (PeriodDeclarer.cs:767-768), and when it is the only key for the source it is the one kept. If the payment later gets a review check, the draft branch at line 342 is skipped because the status is not Draft. The else-branch at 348-353 then reports the line as Kept. The same happens in the totals cut at 383-388.
