# SatellesTSD — sixth round: a bug hunt and a check of the tax rules (2026-11-23, version 0.36.0 released, commit 526e573 not released)

**How it was made.** One workflow, two arms, eleven Opus finders and an Opus refuter for every finding. *The bug hunt* covered the work since the fifth audit: the owner's three points of 526e573 (the offer after an import, the run summary, the start without its store; store schema v14), the fifth audit's fixes (its critical and majors, its minors and nits, the leftovers), the Estonian texts, the way from the computed taxes to the declared file, and a sweep of the whole monthly flow. *The tax-rules check* covered the rates, thresholds and base rules of 2026 against EMTA's pages, every payment type's flags against MTA's matrix, the special persons (pension age, non-residents, A1, several contracts, pauses), and two scenario runs in which the finder worked each case out by hand from the law and compared the engine's figures to the cent (residents; non-residents, A1, treaty rates and corrections), with probes over millions of net amounts for the gross-up. The finders were read-only, ran their probes in copies of their own, never opened the real data, and were told to skip what the five earlier audits record as fixed, refused or settled. For a tax finding the refuter checked the legal claim itself where it could reach a source. 14 findings were raised, 2 refuted, 12 confirmed: 2 critical, 0 major, 9 minor, 1 nit; two of the minors (items 6 and 12) are one issue seen from two areas. Item 4 (unpaid leave) was fixed the same day on the owner's word, item 68 of the plan's owner's list; the rest wait for the owner's word.

**What the law was checked against.** EMTA's own pages (emta.ee: the rates, the 2026 changes, the unemployment insurance premium, the exemption at pension age, the suspension of employment in the register, the TSD examples 4, 12 and 16) and the repository's references (MTA's matrix of 18.11.2025, the XML reference). Riigi Teataja's consolidated texts did not load for the agents, so no section of an act was read directly: where a finding rests on a section's wording, that is said with it.

Severity: critical = wrong tax or declaration data reaching the file, or data loss; major = a flow the accountant needs that breaks or misleads; minor = a wrong message or an edge case with a workaround; nit = cosmetic.

## The short list — distinct issues that matter

1. **Unpaid leave registered as a pause drops the social-tax minimum** (item 4, critical). The program takes every paused day of a TÖR entry out of the minimum (decision 89), whatever the reason of the pause. EMTA says unpaid leave of a whole calendar month or longer is registered as a pause, and that the employer still owes social tax on the monthly base (886 in 2026, 292.38) for it. So a month paused for unpaid leave gets no zero line, and a month partly paused gets a share of the minimum, with a note saying the minimum is not owed. The pause's reason is in the export and is only shown in a tooltip today. The workaround is the card's "kaasa". The other pause reasons (parental leave, military service) are not contested. This amends decision 89: the owner's rule is needed.
2. **The employer's III-pillar contribution (type 25) is taxed without its tax-free part** (item 1, critical). The law lets the employer pay into the employee's III pillar free of income tax up to 15 % of the year's income-taxed payments and 6000 a year, declared as MVT_640. The program marks type 25 as "MVT_640 lubatud" in the rules table but applies nothing: income tax is withheld on the whole contribution, and worked back from the net the gross, the unemployment premium and the social tax come out too high as well (EMTA's example 12: 20.59 of income tax where the program gives 86.59). It arises only when the accountant picks type 25 by hand; nothing assigns it.
3. **A month reopened with nothing to change cannot leave Draft** (item 7, minor). "Ava parandamiseks" is offered on an Exported or Uploaded month; with no change, no draft is built, and then export, confirm, withdraw and "Loobu parandamisest" are all closed.
4. **The two cuts can differ by euros, not cents** (item 5, minor). In the totals cut each type is dated by its latest payment, so the month's exemption can land on another type than in the per-payment cut; types with different withholdings then give a different month. The law prescribes no order, so neither figure is wrong, but decision 78 says "cents".
5. **Two defaults that leave pay undeclared until the accountant acts** (items 2 and 3, minor): a row worded "…hüvitis" on termination falls into the codeless Kompensatsioon; TÖR types 504 and 603 have no default payment type.
6. The rest (items 6 and 12, one issue: no search offered when a file already stored is newly linked to a searched month; items 8–11: four texts).

## Tax rules: the rates, thresholds and base rules

### 1. [critical] Type 25 (employer's III-pillar contribution) is taxed with no MVT_640 tax-free part: income tax on the whole contribution, and from a net amount the gross, TK, TTK and SM are too high as well

tax-rule — `SatellesTSD.Core/Tax/TaxEngine.cs:95`

The finding is real. I could not refute it. The law: the employer may pay into an employee's III pillar free of income tax up to 15 % of the income-taxed payments made to the employee in the calendar year, counted cumulatively from 1 January, and at most 6000 euros a year. The free part is declared on TSD lisa 1 as tax-free income type 640, and income tax is withheld only on the excess. EMTA's own example 12 gives this for January: type 10 gross 1600, TK 25.60, MVT 700, TM 192.37, net 1382.03; type 25 gross 400, SM 132.00, TK 6.40, TTK 3.20, 640 = 300.00, TM (400 - 6.40 - 300) x 22 % = 20.59, net 373.01. The repo agrees: docs/reference/apa-xml-reference.md:325 and the rules KR_KOOND_01/06 at :905-906.

The code: SatellesTSD.Core/Tax/PaymentTypes.cs:29 marks type 25 with ThirdPillarExemptionAllowed = true. The only reader of that flag is the rules display at SatellesTSD/RulesDialog.cs:154, which therefore tells the accountant "MVT_640 lubatud" although nothing applies it. EffectiveFlags.Of (TaxEngine.cs:95-99) only ever picks Mvt610 or Mvt650. Withholdings (TaxEngine.cs:241-265) taxes gross - KP - TK - MVT_610/650. GrossFromNet (TaxEngine.cs:224-232) has no 640 term. The Mvt640 constant at DeclarationModel.cs:126 is never written.

Type 25 can be chosen in the payments grid, which lists every type (MainForm.cs:2525-2526). It is never assigned automatically, so the case arises when the accountant picks it.

Figures recomputed by hand, January, no II pillar, the salary using the whole 700 of MVT_610:
- From gross 400: the app gives TM (400 - 6.40) x 22 % = 86.59; the law gives 20.59. That is 66.00 too much income tax in the file.
- From the net 373.01 actually paid to the fund (the app's usual input): GrossFromNet gives 373.01 / 0.76752 = 486.00. The app then gives TK 7.78, TTK 3.89, TM 105.21 and SM 160.38. The law gives gross 400.00, TK 6.40, TTK 3.20, TM 20.59 and SM 132.00.

Nothing in e-MTA's checks catches this, because no MVT_640 block is sent at all. Applying leftover MVT_610 to type 25 is not itself a defect: matrix column V allows 610/650 on type 25.

No numbered decision (1-253) and none of the five earlier audits mentions MVT_640 or type 25. Plan line 99 only states the 15 % / 6000 rule.

On severity: wrong income tax, and from a net amount also wrong gross, TK and SM, reach the declaration file whenever type 25 is used. By the definitions that is critical, not major. Its reach is limited to the accountant choosing type 25 by hand.

**Fix:** Smallest sound fix, now: refuse type 25 in TaxEngine.Calculate with a coded Estonian refusal (a new PaymentChecks code), or leave it out of the grid's type list and the Calculator's type list. The message should say that the MVT_640 part (15 % of the year's income-taxed payments, at most 6000 a year) is not computed and the payment must be declared by hand. Also stop RulesDialog showing "lubatud" for MVT_640 until it is applied.

Full fix, later: give TaxInput a Cap640. Compute it as min(15 % x (the income-taxed payments to the person at this payer from 1 January to date, this payment included), 6000) minus the 640 already used this year. That needs the earlier months from the store. Deduct it after MVT_610 in Withholdings (tmBase = gross - kp - tk - mvt610 - mvt640, floored at 0). Add the r x cap640 term in GrossFromNet. Because the 15 % base includes the contribution itself, solve the net-to-gross step for that, or iterate it. Write an MVT_640 amount block, with 1160_640 in the Excel export.

## Tax rules by computation: residents

**What the finder checked and could not verify.** Method: I exported HEAD (526e573) SatellesTSD.Core to (a temporary copy) and wrote a console probe there ((a temporary copy)). For each scenario it starts from a gross, works out every figure by hand, derives the net, sends that net through MonthAllocator/TaxEngine with TaxRates.Year2026 and compares the results to the cent. Rates I checked against emta.ee (the Maksumäärad page and the page on the exemption at pension age) all match TaxRates.Year2026:

- social tax 33 % on a minimum base of 886 (292.38 a month)

The pension-age table in PensionAge.cs matches the RPKS § 7 schedule: 63 years 3 months for those born in 1954, rising three months a year to 65 for 1961.

My hand formulas, worked from the gross G:
- KP = round(G × p), TK = round(G × 0.016), TTK = round(G × 0.008)

- SM = round((G + top-up) × 0.33), where top-up = round(886 × share) − the month's code-10/33 base (SMS § 2 lg 2–3)

## From the computed taxes to the declared file

### 5. [minor] Totals cut: each type is dated by its latest payment, so the allocator spends the month's exemption in a different order and the cut switch changes the month's gross and taxes by euros, not the cents decision 78 states

bug — `SatellesTSD.Core/Declare/PeriodDeclarer.cs:453`

Confirmed in the code that runs. In the totals cut, PeriodDeclarer.BuildPerson (SatellesTSD.Core/Declare/PeriodDeclarer.cs:451-454) makes one MonthPayment per type. It carries the summed net and the date `payments.Max(p => p.Date!.Value)` (line 453). MonthAllocator.Allocate (SatellesTSD.Core/Tax/MonthAllocator.cs:112-127) sorts the lines by that date and spends the remaining exemption on them in that order. The class summary (lines 42-43) says the exemption is "consumed in date order". So a type whose last payment is late in the month gets the exemption after a type paid earlier, even when its first payment came first. In the per-payment cut (PeriodDeclarer.cs:459-462) each payment keeps its own date.

I checked the first scenario's arithmetic by hand from the net-to-gross formula for type 10 (net = 0.75192*gross + 0.22*E, with TK 1.6 %, II pillar 2 %, TM 22 %):
- Exemption 700 on the salary: gross 1790.08, sick pay gross 128.21. Month gross 1918.29 or 1918.30.
- Exemption 600 on the salary and 100 on the sick pay: gross 1819.34 + 100.00 = 1919.34.
- Social tax differs by about 0.33 × 29.26 = 9.65. This matches the finder's probe: taxes of 923.35 per payment against 934.27 in the totals cut.

The gap is not a rounding effect. Grossing up is linear for a fixed exemption per type, so with the same exemption split both cuts would agree to the cent. The whole difference comes from the exemption moving between types with different withholdings: type 24 has no SM, TK or KP, and type 21 has no TK.

Decision 78 (plan line 633) prescribes a sum per type, "the month's exemption and minimum allocated over the types", and states that the figures "may differ in cents from the per-payment cut". The owner's item 3 (plan line 476) prescribes the latest date on the line. No decision prescribes the allocation order. The decision's stated premise (cents) is false for this case, so the decision does not settle it. No earlier audit records it: audit-2026-11-05 item at line 259 is about kept lines, a different issue.

Law: TuMS § 23 gives the basic exemption as a monthly amount, and § 42 has the payer apply it when withholding. No order across payments within the month is prescribed. Both figure sets are therefore lawful declarations of the same net payments, and neither is a wrong tax in the legal sense. The defects are two. First, a setting presented as a presentation choice (decision 212) changes the gross and the taxes sent to MTA by euros. Second, the decision the owner approved misstates that trade-off. Minor: a misleading result with a workaround (use the per-payment cut), and no unlawful figure reaches the file.

**Fix:** Smallest sound fix: in the totals cut, first run MonthAllocator over the individual payments in date order, as the per-payment cut does. Then give each type's line the exemption its own payments used (the sum of their ExemptionUsed) as its cap, and compute the type line on its summed net with that cap. Keep the line dated by the latest payment, as item 3 requires. Grossing up is linear for a fixed exemption, so both cuts then agree to within per-line rounding. Alternatively, add up the per-payment results per type, as the finder proposes. Apply the minimum top-up afterwards, on the last carrying line, as now.

If the owner prefers to keep the current behaviour, amend decision 78. Its wording should say that the exemption follows each type's latest date, so the month's gross and taxes may differ by euros from the per-payment cut, not cents.
